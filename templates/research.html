Research

Measuring latent economic quantities from observed choices

How much of a household’s resources reaches each member, and how should time devoted to care be valued? My work addresses these questions through models that connect observed consumption, labor supply, and time allocation to individual resource shares and the value of caregiving time. The papers below study resource allocation in one-adult households, the incorporation of children into labor-supply models, and the full cost of children. Current projects extend this agenda to remote work, care for older parents, and post-divorce resource allocation. A further project examines how commodity taxation and trade costs shape firm location, skilled wages, and welfare.
