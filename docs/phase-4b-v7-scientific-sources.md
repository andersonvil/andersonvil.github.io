# Phase 4B v7 — scientific source map

This internal map records where the public-facing statements on the three research pages come from. It is not rendered by the site. The manuscripts remain the authoritative sources; the web copy is a concise, non-technical synthesis.

## Children Costs in a One-Adult Household

Source: `C:\dev\andersonvil-academic-os-sources\03-research\paper-1.pdf`, version 12 January 2026.

- Research question and contribution: abstract and introduction; extension of collective consumption to one-adult households.
- Economic model: Sections 2–3; Beckerian altruism, consumption technology, sharing rule, assignable adult clothing.
- Identification and econometrics: Section 3 and empirical specification; preference stability, exclusion restrictions, simultaneous nonlinear demand equations, 2SLS, and matching sensitivity.
- Data: Section 4 and Table 2; harmonized FES/EFS/LCF, 1978–2022; final sample of 40,403 households. Web table values are a subset of manuscript Table 2.
- Findings: introduction and results tables; one-child shares of 14.7% for mothers, 22.2% for fathers, and 14.2% in the couple benchmark. The site explicitly notes that the mother–father difference is not statistically established.
- Limits: maintained preference stability and exclusions, clothing zeros, small father sample, fertility selection, and the absence of a causal policy design.

## How to Incorporate Children into Labor Supply Equations?

Source: `C:\dev\andersonvil-academic-os-sources\03-research\paper-2.pdf`, version 4 September 2026. The PDF title contains “Childen”; the site records the divergence and uses the corrected canonical title.

- Research question and contribution: abstract and introduction; children enter through time and monetary mechanisms rather than demographic controls.
- Economic model: Sections 2–3, especially the tractable labor-supply representation reproduced on the site. The web explanation distinguishes the effective-resource income effect from the time/availability effect.
- Identification: nonlinear baseline identification result, linear partial-identification case, and the parametric extension for nonparticipants.
- Econometrics: Section 4; quadratic Brown–Levin–Ulph baseline, control-function maximum likelihood, wage and expenditure instruments, participation censoring, and simulated errors.
- Data: Section 4 and Table 1; 2019 PSID, 1,270 single women, including 987 childless women and 283 mothers. Web table values are a subset of Table 1.
- Findings: Tables 4–5 and conclusion; 497 annual childcare hours in the benchmark, 389-hour ATUS comparator, and the reported cost distributions. The site labels these as model-based estimates, not compensation recommendations.
- Limits: small mother sample, young-child/single-woman population, reservation-wage valuation for nonparticipants, and mechanical square-root-of-two scaling for two children.

## The Household Demand for Leisure, the Price of Time and the Full Cost of Children

Source: `C:\dev\andersonvil-academic-os-sources\03-research\paper-3.pdf`, version 18 September 2026.

- Research question and contribution: abstract and introduction; endogenous productive price of parental time plus monetary, time, and full child costs.
- Economic model: Sections 2–3; collective household model, childcare production, full leisure, and productive/experienced components of parental time.
- Identification: Section 3 and supplied EEA slides 10–11; substitution between parental and purchased care for the time price, and Rothbarth-style full-leisure compensation under preference stability and sharing-rule invariance for monetary cost.
- Econometrics: Section 4; recursive childcare equations, 3SLS full-leisure demand, softplus monetary cost, and ridge regularization.
- Data: Section 4, Tables 1–2; 2019 PSID, 825 dual-earner couples, including 463 with young children and 362 without. Web table values are a subset of Table 2.
- Childcare-time adjustment: manuscript page 25 and Table 1. The site reproduces `tᵢᵃᵈʲ = tᵢᴾˢᴵᴰ − kᵢ × tanh(tᵢᴾˢᴵᴰ / kᵢ)`, its zero-preserving and large-report behavior, and the adjusted means of 9.66 hours for fathers and 18.97 for mothers.
- Findings: Table 6 and Section 4.2.3; productive shares 0.44 (SE 0.26) for mothers and 0.35 (SE 0.21) for fathers; median one-child time cost about $230/week versus about $350 under wage valuation. Specification-sensitive monetary and total costs are not promoted as headline findings.
- Limits: dual-earner selection, young-child scope, retrospective/broad time measurement, time/spending reference-period mismatch, formal-care rationing, weak monetary identification, ridge regularization, and 2017 robustness sensitivity.

## Editorial safeguards

- Exact manuscript abstracts remain unchanged.
- Every non-technical overview is 120–180 words.
- Descriptive tables are explicitly labelled as selected manuscript statistics.
- No web sentence upgrades an association or model estimate into a causal or normative policy claim.
- Production remains blocked until the author approves wording, files, statuses, image rights, and distribution rights.
