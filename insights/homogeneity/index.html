1. Intuition and a numerical example

A household with a monthly income of €2,000 chooses a basket. Suppose every price and its income double. A basket xxx was affordable before if p⋅x≤2000p\cdot x\le 2000p⋅x≤2000⁠; it is affordable now if 2p⋅x≤40002p\cdot x\le 40002p⋅x≤4000⁠, which is the same inequality. The budget set has not moved, so a household that cares about quantities, not about nominal values, has no reason to change its basket. Demand is homogeneous of degree zero in prices and income: this is the formal definition of “no money illusion”.

The econometrician observes JJJ prices and income YYY and writes the demand for good 1 as

log⁡q1=β0+∑j=1Jβjlog⁡pj+βJ+1log⁡Y+u.(1)\log q_1=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\beta_j\log p_j+\beta_{J+1}\log Y+u . \qquad \htmlId{equation-1}{(1)}logq1​=β0​+j=1∑J​βj​logpj​+βJ+1​logY+u.(1)

Section 3 shows that multiplying all prices and income by λ>0\lambda>0λ>0 multiplies q1q_1q1​ by λθ\lambda^{\theta}λθ⁠, where θ≡∑j=1Jβj+βJ+1\theta\equiv\sum_{j=1}^J\beta_j+\beta_{J+1}θ≡∑j=1J​βj​+βJ+1​⁠. With θ=0.1\theta=0.1θ=0.1⁠, doubling prices and income raises demand by 20.1−1=7.2%2^{0.1}-1=7.2\%20.1−1=7.2%⁠, although nothing real has changed: that is money illusion. Homogeneity is the restriction θ=0\theta=0θ=0⁠.

Homogeneity is therefore a consequence of the budget constraint, not an assumption about the shape of preferences. It requires only that UUU depends on quantities and not on nominal magnitudes, and that the maximiser is unique (otherwise the demand set is homogeneous).

The cost of imposing. Figure 3 illustrates (22) and (23). Without common inflation, nominal prices are uncorrelated (corr=0.00\mathrm{corr}=0.00corr=0.00), yet imposing a true restriction cuts the standard deviation of β^1\hat{\beta}_1β^​1​ from 0.0590.0590.059 to 0.0490.0490.049 (ratio 0.830.830.83⁠; (23) gives 0.830.830.83). Precisely when imposing homogeneity buys precision, the data say least about whether it holds.

Conclusion. Zero-degree homogeneity is not an assumption on preferences but a consequence of the budget constraint, and in a log-linear demand it reduces to one linear restriction, θ=0\theta=0θ=0⁠. Test it as the coefficient on log⁡pJ\log p_JlogpJ​ in the reparametrised regression (25) and report its confidence interval. Impose it knowing that the gain (23) and the risk (22) are governed by the same correlation between β^j\hat{\beta}_jβ^​j​ and θ^\hat{\theta}θ^⁠.
